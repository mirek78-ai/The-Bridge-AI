The challenge

AI readiness is not a hardware purchase.

Enterprises face pressure to move from AI pilots to production while workload demand, operational maturity, storage requirements and facilities constraints are still evolving. A capable fabric is only one part of a supportable AI platform.

Uncertain demand

Inference, RAG, fine-tuning and distributed training have different infrastructure implications.

Shared dependencies

Storage, security, power, cooling, data paths and people determine real outcomes.

Operational exposure

Lifecycle, recovery, observability and skills matter after the first deployment.

Investment risk

Buy and expand when business demand and performance evidence justify scale.

The signature framework

Eight stages. One continuous governance lifecycle.

Each stage has accountable decisions, measurable evidence and clear exit criteria. The cycle repeats as workloads and technology change.

01 / STRATEGY

Align

Business outcomes, AI use cases, risk and ownership.

02 / BASELINE

Assess

Current state, dependencies, readiness and gaps.

03 / CHOICE

Architect

Compare options; choose lowest justified complexity.

04 / EVIDENCE

Validate

Lab, physical pilots, failure and security tests.

05 / CHANGE

Deploy

Controlled waves, rollback and operational handoff.

06 / ASSURANCE

Operate

Observe, patch, secure, recover and manage capacity.

07 / LEARNING

Improve

Optimize, automate and adopt proven capabilities.

08 / EXPANSION

Scale

Grow only with validated demand and readiness.

Governing principleThe lifecycle is continuous. Production deployment is a milestone, not the end of architecture responsibility.
Architecture domains

Four coordinated layers—not one oversized fabric.

Enterprise front-end

Applications, API services, general connectivity, management and shared services.

AI back-end (optional)

Dedicated GPU east-west connectivity only where workload characteristics justify it.

Storage and data

Data ingestion, shared storage, checkpoints, data protection and recovery.

Cross-cutting controls

Security, resilience, observability, automation, lifecycle and skills.

A dedicated AI back-end is conditional and workload-driven. VXLAN EVPN is not mandated for every AI use case.

Adoption discipline

Foundation first. Conditional capabilities when proven.

FOUNDATIONAL

Reliable from day one

Segmentation, independent management, vulnerability governance, observability, recovery, documentation and ownership.

CONDITIONAL

Justified by workload

Dedicated AI back-end, RoCEv2, EVPN Multi-Site, advanced microsegmentation and specialized storage paths.

EMERGING

Governed exploration

Agentic operations, autonomous workflows and advanced remediation—with human review and tested limits.
